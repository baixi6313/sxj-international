# Which Political Parties and Regimes Can Use Shixianjian (SXJ)

> A comprehensive taxonomy from the perspective of Co-creationism

---

## Core Principle

Shixianjian is not a platform *for* any political ideology. It is a **verification protocol**—like TCP/IP or the metric system—that any regime, party, or governance entity can adopt, fork, or interface with.

The question is not *who is allowed* to use it. The question is *what happens to them* when they do—or when they refuse.

---

## I. Liberal Democracies (Multi-Party Systems)

### Examples
Nordic model states, Westminster systems, federal republics (U.S., Germany, India), parliamentary democracies (Japan, South Korea).

### How They Use SXJ
- **Opposition parties** use it to archive government failures (wage arrears, environmental violations, welfare gaps) with tamper-proof timestamps.
- **Ruling parties** use it to verify campaign promises—"We pledged X; here is the SXJ record showing X was delivered."
- **Civil society** uses it to cross-check both sides.

### Strategic Outcome
SXJ becomes a **non-partisan public ledger** of political accountability. The party that ratifies the most citizen claims gains credibility; the party that blocks verification loses it.

---

## II. Authoritarian / Single-Party Regimes

### Examples
One-party states, dominant-party systems, military juntas, theocratic autocracies.

### How They Use SXJ
This is the most paradoxical case. Authoritarian regimes have the strongest incentive to *block* SXJ, because SXJ archives facts they cannot delete.

However, if they choose to interface with SXJ:
- **Selective ratification**: Ratify low-sensitivity claims (infrastructure, environmental data) to gain international legitimacy, while ignoring high-sensitivity ones (political prisoners, labor rights).
- **Forking**: Create a state-controlled mirror of SXJ that uses the same protocol but censors at the input layer.

### Strategic Outcome
A forked SXJ is not SXJ. It is a dead chain. The global Gzz network will not recognize censored forks, because validation requires cross-jurisdictional ratification. **The regime that forks SXJ proves it fears the truth; the regime that refuses to fork proves it fears transparency even more.**

---

## III. Hybrid / Transitional Regimes

### Examples
Semi-authoritarian states, post-conflict nations, regimes undergoing democratic backsliding (e.g., Hungary, Turkey, Venezuela, Myanmar).

### How They Use SXJ
These regimes are SXJ's **early adopters**.
- **Anti-corruption campaigns**: New leaders use SXJ to document predecessor crimes with immutable evidence.
- **Transitional justice**: Victims archive atrocities; SXJ provides the verification layer for truth commissions.
- **Electoral integrity**: Opposition and ruling parties both submit claims; SXJ ratifies which claims are factual.

### Strategic Outcome
SXJ acts as a **stabilizer** during power transitions. It prevents the new regime from simply replacing old lies with new ones, because the archive persists across governments.

---

## IV. Failed States / Ungoverned Territories

### Examples
Post-collapse nations, regions under warlord control, territories with no functioning central government (e.g., parts of Somalia, Syria, Libya, Afghanistan).

### How They Use SXJ
In the absence of a state, SXJ becomes the **default governance infrastructure**:
- **Clans / tribes / militias** use SXJ to negotiate resource disputes—"Our water rights claim was ratified by three neighboring communities."
- **Humanitarian NGOs** use SXJ to verify aid distribution, preventing theft by local strongmen.
- **Citizens** use SXJ to build a shadow civil registry—births, deaths, property claims—that survives state collapse.

### Strategic Outcome
SXJ does not need a state to function. In failed states, it becomes the **sole source of social credit**. The warlord who recognizes SXJ claims gains legitimacy; the one who burns the records loses it.

---

## V. Sub-State / Local Governments

### Examples
City-states, provincial governments, autonomous regions, mayoral administrations.

### How They Use SXJ
Local governments are SXJ's **most pragmatic users**.
- **Mayors** ratify citizen claims about local services (garbage collection, road repair, school quality) to build a verifiable track record.
- **Provincial governments** use SXJ to bypass central bureaucracy—"We delivered welfare; here is the SXJ record."
- **Special economic zones** adopt SXJ as a governance tool to attract international investment.

### Strategic Outcome
SXJ enables **federalism by protocol**. Local governments that perform well on SXJ gain Gzz credit, which translates into cross-border investment and talent attraction, independent of central government approval.

---

## VI. Exile Governments / Non-State Actors

### Examples
Governments-in-exile, liberation movements, diaspora parliaments, indigenous self-governance bodies.

### How They Use SXJ
- **Exile governments** use SXJ to maintain a parallel civil registry for citizens who cannot access the home country's system.
- **Indigenous nations** use SXJ to archive land claims, treaty violations, and ecological stewardship with global verification.
- **Liberation movements** use SXJ to document state crimes, creating a prosecution-ready evidence chain for future international tribunals.

### Strategic Outcome
SXJ gives non-state actors **sovereignty-grade infrastructure** without needing territorial control. An exile government that maintains a verified SXJ archive is more legitimate, in the Gzz network, than a occupying regime that does not.

## VII. Supranational / International Organizations

### Examples
UN agencies, regional blocs (EU, AU, ASEAN), international courts (ICC, ICJ), multilateral development banks.

### How They Use SXJ
- **UNDP / UNHCR**: Use SXJ to verify refugee claims, replacing paper-based vulnerability assessments.
- **International Criminal Court**: Use SXJ archives as admissible evidence—tamper-proof, time-stamped, multi-party ratified.
- **World Bank / IMF**: Use SXJ to verify loan conditions were met, or to document human rights impacts of structural adjustment.
- **EU**: Use SXJ to verify cross-border labor rights, environmental compliance, and asylum claims.

### Strategic Outcome
SXJ becomes the **technical implementation layer** of international law. Organizations that adopt SXJ gain operational efficiency; organizations that ignore it lose credibility as fact-finders.

## VIII. Political Parties (Across All Regime Types)

### Ruling Parties
Use SXJ to **verify delivery** of campaign promises. A ruling party with a high ratification rate becomes electorally unbeatable—not because of propaganda, but because of verified performance.

### Opposition Parties
Use SXJ to **document failures** of the ruling regime. An opposition party that archives citizen grievances with SXJ builds a prosecution-ready case for the next election—or the next revolution.

### New / Minor Parties
Use SXJ to **prove competence** at the local level. A minor party that delivers verified results in one municipality can export its Gzz credit to other regions, bypassing traditional media gatekeeping.

### Revolutionary Parties
Use SXJ to **pre-build governance infrastructure** before taking power. A revolutionary movement with a functioning SXJ archive is not a mob; it is a government-in-waiting with a verified track record.

## IX. Corporations and Private Actors (Quasi-Political)

While not "regimes" in the traditional sense, multinational corporations, platform monopolies, and even criminal organizations operate as **de facto governance entities** in many regions.

### How They Interface with SXJ
- **Corporations**: Use SXJ to verify ESG claims, supply chain labor rights, and carbon offsets. A corporation with verified SXJ records gains market access; one without faces divestment.
- **Platform monopolies**: SXJ replaces their internal content moderation with a public verification protocol, forcing them to cede narrative control.
- **Criminal organizations**: They cannot use SXJ, because SXJ's distributed ratification makes corruption and coercion visible. **SXJ is the one protocol they cannot game.**
